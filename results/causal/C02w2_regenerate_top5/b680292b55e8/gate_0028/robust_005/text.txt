This study introduces two innovative methods for multi-organ segmentation using deep learning. Both techniques utilize the concept of leveraging multiple input planes as parallel channels, allowing different supervision schemes to be defined for each plane. This approach enables the information flow to better handle ambiguous signals from partial or irrelevant annotations (semi-supervision) and to enhance generalization to test images with occluded or rotated organs. The study combines a convolutional autoencoder for RGB channels to extract features and a 3D ResNet for T2w/FLAIR subtraction to convey anatomical details. This approach enables the efficient representation of both appearance and geometry using complementary domains, providing significant performance improvements over standard methods. The study's results show marked improvements over seminal MICCAI publications, with a quantitative comparison on five public datasets demonstrating that the proposed methods exceed state-of-the-art scores, achieving results comparable to human experts with a simple modification of the backbone architecture.
