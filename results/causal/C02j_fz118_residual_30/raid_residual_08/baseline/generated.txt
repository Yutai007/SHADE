Rue Royale (English: Royal Street) is one of the oldest and most famous streets in Lyon, France. Located in the 1st arrondissement, it stretches from Place des Terreaux to Place Bellecour. Known for its historic buildings, including the Hôtel de Ville (City Hall), the Théâtre des Célestins, and the Basilique de Notre-Dame de Fourvière.

Originally built in the 16th century as part of Francis I's plans to modernize Lyon, the street has been the site of significant events, such as the signing of the Edict of Nantes in 1598, which granted religious toleration to France's Protestants. During the French Revolution, it was the site of public executions, and in the 19th century, it became a center for Lyon's silk industry.

Today, Rue Royale is a popular destination for tourists and locals, who come to admire its architecture and stroll along its length. The street is also home to numerous shops, restaurants, and cafes, making it a vibrant part of Lyon's bustling city center.
