Rue Royale (English: Royal Street) is one of the oldest and most famous streets in Lyon, France. Located in the 1st arrondissement, it stretches from Place des Terreaux to Place Bellecour. Known for its historic buildings, Rue Royale includes the Hôtel de Ville (City Hall), the Théâtre des Célestins, and the Basilique de Notre-Dame de Fourvière.

Originally built in the 16th century for Francis I's modernization of Lyon, the street has hosted significant events, such as the signing of the Edict of Nantes in 1598, which granted religious toleration to France's Protestants. It has also been the site of public executions and the center of Lyon's silk industry in the 19th century.

Today, Rue Royale is a popular destination for tourists and locals, with its architecture, shops, and restaurants making it a vibrant part of Lyon's city center.
