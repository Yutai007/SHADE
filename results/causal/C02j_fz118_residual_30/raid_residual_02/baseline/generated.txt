The Science of Discworld blends two parallel storylines: one in the fantastical Discworld, a flat planet with four elephants and a giant turtle, and the other in the real world, focusing on a science experiment at the Unseen University in Ankh-Morpork. In Discworld, wizards accidentally create a universe within a small bottle, leading to the realization that the Discworld itself is a random experiment by the Unseen University's wizards. They discover the underlying principles governing their universe and the power of narrative and belief.

Meanwhile, in the real world, renowned science writer Ian Stewart and popular science author Jack Cohen examine how scientific concepts are represented in Discworld. As they explore the Discworld, they explain complex scientific ideas in an accessible way, using examples from both worlds to illustrate their points. The authors delve into subjects like evolution, biology, cosmology, and culture, offering a fresh perspective on how science and fiction can inform and enrich each other. They challenge common scientific paradigms, exploring the role of imagination, the significance of stories, and the impact of belief systems in shaping our understanding of the universe.

The Science of Discworld seamlessly weaves together an imaginative narrative with real-world scientific explanations, entertaining and enlightening readers on the importance of scientific inquiry and the intricacies of the universe we inhabit. This compelling and thought-provoking novel encourages readers to embrace the wonder of both science and fiction, as each holds its own unique power to shape our view of the world.
