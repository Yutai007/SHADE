This study examines the time complexity of synchronizing a collection of discrete pulse-coupled oscillators (PCOs) on a tree topology. PCOs have been used to model and control biological systems, such as heartbeat regulation and animal locomotion. However, the effectiveness of these networks is heavily dependent on the ability to synchronize the individual oscillators, which remains an open research challenge. Despite extensive research on this topic, it has not yet been established what determines the efficiency of different synchronization algorithms.

In this context, the study analyzes two popular algorithms for synchronization: the Kuramoto algorithm and its variants based on spectral theory. These algorithms have a linear time complexity, which allows for easy scaling to handle large numbers of coupled oscillators. The study demonstrates how the convergence properties of these algorithms depend on various parameters of the system, such as coupling strength and initial conditions, using both numerical simulations and theoretical analyses. The results suggest that the efficiency of these algorithms requires careful tuning of these parameters. Additionally, the study observes that the performance of these algorithms is also influenced by the structural characteristics of the network, such as clustering coefficients and path lengths.
