This study examines the time complexity of synchronizing a collection of discrete pulse-coupled oscillators (PCOs) on a tree topology. PCOs have been used to model and control biological systems, such as heartbeat regulation and animal locomotion. However, their effectiveness is heavily dependent on the ability to synchronize the individual oscillators, which remains an open research challenge. Despite extensive work in this area, it has not yet been established what determines the efficiency of different algorithms in accomplishing this task.

In this context, we analyze two popular algorithms for synchronization: the Kuramoto algorithm and its variants based on spectral theory. Both these algorithms are known to operate with linear time complexity, which implies that they can be easily scaled up to handle large numbers of coupled oscillators. We demonstrate how their convergence properties depend on various parameters of the system (such as coupling strength and initial conditions), using both numerical simulations and theoretical analyses. Our results suggest that the efficient operation of these algorithms requires careful tuning of these parameter values. Additionally, we observe that certain structural characteristics of the network (e.g., clustering coefficients and path lengths) also play a crucial role in determining their performance.
