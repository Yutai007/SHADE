The BBC has learned that additional cases of foot and mouth disease were confirmed in England on Wednesday, prompting calls for stricter rules on livestock.

Defra confirmed the latest cases in Surrey, stating it was "likely" more cases would be found. This comes days after the UK's chief veterinary officer declared the country technically disease-free.

On Wednesday, officials confirmed that two more sites in Surrey - a farm in Wormley and another in Ockley - were linked to a site in nearby Felbridge, where a case of the virus was discovered last week.

Suede yesterday's daily essentials expressed reassurance that the virus posed no threat to food safety or the public.

However, the disease poses a significant threat to the livestock industry, with farmers disposing of suspected infected animals to prevent the disease's spread, disrupting the supply chain and costing the industry billions.

The National Farmers' Union (NFU) has called for a compulsory livestock vaccination program to prevent the spread of the disease and protect the rural economy.

NFU's chief livestock advisor, Dr. Ceris Jones, said: "There is a clear solution to this recurring disruption, which is to introduce a compulsory vaccination program for all livestock susceptible to foot and mouth disease."

Dr. Jones added that every time the country has an outbreak, "the impact is massive, both in terms of costs to the taxpayer and the price paid by farmers."

He said a compulsory vaccination program would introduce "a disease status that provides clarity and confidence to farmers, Government, and the wider public."

Dr. Jones said the NFU had submitted its proposal to the government and was "eager to discuss it further."

A government spokesperson stated: "We have robust measures in place to prevent the spread of foot and mouth disease, including regular testing and a comprehensive vaccination program."

"We take the safety and security of our food and agriculture system extremely seriously and remain determined to eradicate this dreadful disease."
