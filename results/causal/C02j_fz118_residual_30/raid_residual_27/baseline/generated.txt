The BBC has learned that additional cases of foot and mouth disease were confirmed in England on Wednesday, prompting calls for stricter rules on livestock.

Defra confirmed the latest cases in Surrey, stating it was "likely" more cases would be found. This comes days after the UK's chief veterinary officer declared the country technically free of foot and mouth disease.

On Wednesday, officials announced that two more sites in Surrey - a farm in Wormley and another in Ockley - had been confirmed as "linked" to a site in nearby Felbridge, where a case of the virus was discovered last week.

The UK's chief veterinary officer expressed reassurance that the virus posed no threat to food safety or public health. However, the disease poses a significant threat to the livestock industry, as farmers often euthanize suspected infected animals to prevent the spread, disrupting supply chains and costing billions.

The National Farmers' Union (NFU) has called for compulsory vaccination of livestock to prevent the spread of the disease and protect the rural economy. NFU's chief livestock advisor, Dr. Ceris Jones, proposed a compulsory vaccination program for all susceptible livestock, stating that it would provide clarity and confidence to farmers, government, and the public.

Dr. Jones emphasized that every time the country experiences an outbreak, the impact is massive, both in terms of costs to the taxpayer and the price paid by farmers. He argued that a compulsory vaccination program would introduce a disease status that would provide clarity and confidence to farmers, government, and the public.

NFU has submitted its proposal to the government and is eager to discuss it further. A government spokesperson stated that they have robust measures in place to prevent the spread of foot and mouth disease, including regular testing and a comprehensive vaccination program. They also emphasized their commitment to eradicating the virus.
