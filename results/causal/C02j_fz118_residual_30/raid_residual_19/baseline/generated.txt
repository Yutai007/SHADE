The European Parliament has ordered a revision of its ambitious plans to create a unified patent system across Europe.

The assembly's legal affairs committee stated that the package, known as the unitary patent, would be rewritten to address critics' concerns.

These included the involvement of the European Court of Justice, the need for translation, and the validity of patents in all EU states.

The legislation aims to simplify patent protection by allowing inventors to gain patent protection in a single, unified patent court.

The legislation has faced resistance from Italy, Spain, and Poland, who have threatened to block the plan unless changes are made.

The committee said it planned to split the unitary patent package into two bills.

One would deal with the unitary patent itself, while the other would establish a new court to handle patent disputes, known as the Unified Patent Court (UPC).

Italian socialist Guido Sacconi, who led the work on the report, said the committee had decided to "take the bull by the horns."

"We have decided to modify the two pillars of the unitary patent package: the unitary patent itself and the Unified Patent Court," he said.

"The two elements will be split, allowing us to proceed with the patent court and a bit later discuss the unitary patent."

The committee's proposal will now go before the full parliament for a vote.

If passed, it will then be up to the EU's 28 member states to decide whether to proceed with the plan.

Currently, inventors must apply for patent protection in each EU country where they want patent protection.
