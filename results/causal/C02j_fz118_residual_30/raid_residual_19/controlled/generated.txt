The European Parliament has ordered a revision of its ambitious plans to create a unified patent system across Europe.

The assembly's legal affairs committee stated that the package, known as the unitary patent, would be rewritten to address critics' concerns.

These included the involvement of the European Court of Justice, the need for translation, and the validity of patents in all EU states.

The legislation aims to simplify patent protection by allowing inventors to gain patent protection in a single, unified patent court.

The legislation faces resistance from Italy, Spain, and Poland, who have threatened to block the plan unless changes are made.

The committee plans to split the unitary patent package into two bills.

One will deal with the unitary patent itself, while the other will establish a new court to handle patent disputes, known as the Unified Patent Court (UPC).

Italian socialist Guido Sacconi, who led the work on the report, said the committee had decided to "take the bull by the horns."

"We have decided to modify the two pillars of the unitary patent package: the unitary patent itself and the Unified Patent Court," he explained.

"Both elements will be divided, which allows us to proceed first with the patent court and later discuss the unitary patent."

The revised proposal will now go before the full parliament for a vote.

If passed, it will then be up to the 28 member states to decide whether to implement the plan.
