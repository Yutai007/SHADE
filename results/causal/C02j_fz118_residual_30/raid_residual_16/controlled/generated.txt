Edwin Bryant (January 1834 – April 4, 1908) was an English-born American businessman and politician who served as the sixth mayor of Los Angeles, California, from 1884 to 1886. He also served as a member of the Los Angeles Common Council, the governing body of the city, from 1872 to 1874 and again from 1878 to 1882.

Bryant was born in Manchester, England, in 1834 and immigrated to the United States with his family in 1846. They settled in Wisconsin, where he received a liberal education and became a naturalized U.S. citizen in 1851.

In 1857, Bryant moved to California and settled in Los Angeles. He founded the Los Angeles Printing Press and served as its editor and publisher. He worked as a journalist and wrote for various newspapers and magazines.

Bryant was elected to the Los Angeles Common Council in 1872 and served two terms before being re-elected in 1878. During his tenure, he advocated for water system development and public transportation expansion. He improved public schools and promoted economic growth.

After retiring from politics, Bryant continued to work as a journalist and businessman. He died in Los Angeles on April 4, 1908, at the age of 74.
