We propose a weakly supervised semantic segmentation method that utilizes large quantities and diversities in unlabeled videos to produce accurate, high-resolution results quickly. Our approach leverages motion cues found in video sequences for object and class localization. These cues are learned by an offline classifier, whose predictions serve as initial seed sets for further propagation via graph cut optimization. This information extraction procedure incorporates both spatial and temporal structural information, yielding superior performance over static image-only learning frameworks. We also incorporate recent work, such as Deep Features (Convolutional Neural Network), to improve overall accuracy and efficiency without significantly increasing runtime costs. Compared to recent state-of-the-art methods like PropNet and LTFS2K+N, our algorithm outperforms them significantly on public benchmark datasets like Cityscapes, MPII, and CamVid, which contain various densely labeled regions and significant variation across different scene categories.
