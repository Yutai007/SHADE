We propose a weakly supervised semantic segmentation method that utilizes large quantities and diversities in unlabeled videos to produce accurate, high-resolution results quickly. Our method utilizes motion cues found in video sequences to localize objects and classes. These cues are learned by an offline classifier, and their predictions are used as initial seed sets to propagate via graph cut optimization. Our method utilizes both spatial and temporal structural information, giving our system a good performance over static image-only learning frameworks as reported in literature. We also incorporate recent work, such as Deep Features (a Convolutional Neural Network), to improve overall accuracy and efficiency without significantly increasing run time cost. Our method significantly outperforms recent state-of-the-art methods such as PropNet and LTFS2K+N on public benchmark datasets such as Cityscapes, MPII, and CamVid, which are densely and significantly varied in their labeled regions.
