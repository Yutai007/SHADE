This paper introduces a novel method for addressing a single erbium ion within a silicon crystal using a focused laser beam. Erbium ions are introduced into the silicon lattice through implantation, and their optical properties make them ideal for quantum information processing and other photonic applications.

Current techniques for addressing individual erbium ions in silicon involve electrical methods, which are limited by the difficulty in precisely controlling the ion's position and electric field strength. Our proposed optical approach offers several advantages, including high spatial resolution, low power consumption, and the ability to address multiple ions simultaneously.

We experimentally demonstrate the ability to optically address a single erbium ion in silicon using a focused laser beam with a radius of approximately 1 micrometer. By tuning the laser frequency to the ion's resonant transition, we observe a clear signature of ion-photon interaction, indicating successful addressing of the individual ion.

Our results open the door for the development of scalable, densely-packed arrays of individually-addressable erbium ions in silicon, which could enable a new generation of quantum information processing and photonic devices. By utilizing the unique properties of rare-earth ions in silicon, this work contributes to the advancement of quantum computing and other photonic applications.
