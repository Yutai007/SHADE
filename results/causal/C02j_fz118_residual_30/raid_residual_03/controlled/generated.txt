The BBC should not be complacent about its future and must not allow its standards to slip, Business Secretary Peter Mandelson has warned.

In a speech to the Royal Television Society, he said the BBC was "the envy of the world" but it must not take its success for granted.

He also said the BBC must not be seen as "on the side of government" and should be "fearless" in journalism.

Mr Mandelson told the RTS Cambridge Convention that the BBC is "envious of the world" and "an important British institution".

However, he cautioned: "The BBC cannot rest on its laurels or let down its journalistic integrity."

Criticism from some quarters, including MPs, over the BBC's handling of the MPs' expenses scandal.

Mr Mandelson defended the BBC's role in exposing the scandal and called it "right" that they did so.

He also defended the decision to screen footage of Queen storming out of a photoshoot with Annie Leibovitz, saying it was in the public interest.

Mr Mandelson used his speech to defend the government's plans to fund super-fast broadband rollout across the UK.

He said the government was not trying to "pick winners" but wanted "diversity and choice" in the market.

Critics have accused the government of "micromanaging" the BBC and favouring BT over other providers.
