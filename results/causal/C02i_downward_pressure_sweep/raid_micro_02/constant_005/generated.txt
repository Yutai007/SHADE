Lorenzo Sumulong Tañada (June 10, 1908 – August 23, 1980) was a Filipino lawyer and politician who served as Senator for the Liberal Party in the Philippines from 1947 to 1965 and again from 1967 until his death in 1980. He also served as Speaker of the House of Representatives during the Second Republic from 1943-1944.

## Early life[edit]

Lorenzo Sumulong Tañada was born on June 10, 1908, in Manila. He was the son of Manuel L. Quezon, second President of the Commonwealth of the Philippines, and Maria Agoncillo Sumulong, daughter of Don Mariano Ponce de Leon y Sumulong, one of the richest men in the country at that time. His mother's family were descendants of Spanish conquistador Juan de Salcedo, while his father's side came from Chinese immigrants.

In 1926, he graduated with honors from Ateneo Municipal High School, now known as Ateneo de Manila University Senior High School. He also earned his Bachelor of Laws degree in 1931, while studying at the University of Santo Tomas. While there, he became president of the university's student council.

On July 1, 1931, he married Carmen Guerrero Nakpil, daughter of Marcelo H. del Pilar, one of the leaders of the Philippine Revolution against Spain. They had three children: Lorenzo III, Jose Conrado, and Cristina.

## Political career[edit]

### First term as senator[edit]

During the Japanese occupation of the Philippines, he joined the resistance movement and helped organize the Makapili guerillas. He also served as Speaker of the House of Representatives during the Second Republic from 1943-1944.
