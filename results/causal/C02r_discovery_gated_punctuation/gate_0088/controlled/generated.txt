Barry Holmes triumphs in comeback victory

Barry Holmes secured a stunning comeback to win the Welsh Open snooker title in Cardiff. The world number 39 trailed 6-2 to Mark Selby in the final, but won eight of the next nine frames to triumph 10-7.

It is the first ranking event success for the 41-year-old from Birmingham, who has now earned £200,000 in prize money this season.

"I'm over the moon, it's been a great week," Holmes said. "I've been working hard on my game and it's nice to get some reward for it."

Selby, the world number two, had been in dominant form throughout the tournament and looked on course for victory when he moved 6-2 ahead. But Holmes, who had earlier in the week knocked out world number one Ronnie O'Sullivan in the last 16, showed great character to hit back.

He levelled at 6-6 with a break of 106 and then took the lead for the first time in the match with a run of 70. Selby responded with a break of 77 to make it 7-7, but Holmes took the next three frames to move within one of victory.

The Leicester player then had a chance to force a decider in the 17th frame, but missed a red with the rest when on 46. Holmes made no mistake with a break of 53 to secure the title.

"I'm gutted to lose, but I've got to take my hat off to Barry, he played unbelievable," Selby said. "I had my chances in the match, but I couldn't take them. I'm sure I'll be back stronger for it."
