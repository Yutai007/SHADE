In this paper, we introduce a novel approach for multi-atlas label fusion using longitudinal images, termed "4D Multi-Atlas Label Fusion" (4D MALF). This method combines traditional multi-atlas label fusion techniques with longitudinal imaging data to enhance image segmentation accuracy and robustness.

Existing multi-atlas label fusion methods typically rely on pre-processed brain atlases registered to target images and then fusing labels from these atlases using various strategies. However, they are limited by their reliance on pre-defined atlas spaces, which may not accurately capture individual variations in brain anatomy or pathology. Additionally, they do not consider longitudinal imaging data, which provides temporal information.

In contrast, 4D MALF leverages longitudinal imaging data to create a more accurate and personalized representation of brain anatomy. We first align images in a dataset to a common space using a robust registration technique, and then propagate labels from a set of reference atlases to the target image. The resulting label fusion is performed in a 4D space that incorporates both cross-sectional and longitudinal information, enabling the capture of complex patterns of brain structure and function over time.

We evaluate 4D MALF using a large dataset of longitudinal MRI scans from patients with Alzheimer's disease. Our results show significant improvements in segmentation accuracy compared to state-of-the-art multi-atlas label fusion methods, demonstrating the effectiveness of integrating longitudinal information into the label fusion process. We also perform ablation studies to analyze the contributions of different components of our method, providing insights into the benefits of using longitudinal imaging data and the importance of combining information across multiple atlases.

Overall, our work has important implications for the analysis of neuroimaging data, particularly in the context of aging and neurodegenerative diseases where longitudinal data is increasingly available. By harnessing the power of longitudinal imaging, 4D MALF offers a more comprehensive and accurate representation of brain anatomy, potentially leading to improved diagnostic and prognostic tools.
