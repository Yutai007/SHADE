Mentone is a small unincorporated community in Newton County, Texas, United States. It is situated at the intersection of Farm to Market Roads 1486 and 3294, about 15 miles north of the county seat, Newton. Mentone was established in the late 1800s as a railroad stop on the Missouri, Kansas and Texas Railway. The community was named after Mentone, a town in the Alpes-Maritimes department in southeastern France.

Throughout its history, Mentone has been primarily an agricultural community, with cotton and grains being the main crops grown in the area. In the early 20th century, Mentone had a population of around 200 residents, with several businesses including a general store, a cotton gin, and a school. The community also had a post office, which operated from 1896 to the late 1960s.

Today, Mentone remains a small, close-knit community with a population of around 50 residents. The community is known for its annual Fourth of July celebration, which includes a parade, a barbecue, and fireworks. Mentone is also home to the Mentone Cemetery, which is the final resting place for many of the community's early residents.
