Friday, 18 November 2005 00.01 GMT

The release of Microsoft's Xbox 360 game Halo 2 has led to a significant increase in broadband traffic in the US, with one internet service provider reporting a 32% surge in downloads.

Time Warner Cable reported a notable increase in traffic on its network on Thursday, coinciding with the release of the game the previous day. According to the company, all types of data usage had risen, with gaming websites experiencing a 44% boost and game downloads themselves up 32%.

Three individuals have been arrested on suspicion of attempted murder after a man was shot in Manchester. The victim, a 20-year-old man, was shot in the abdomen in Moss Side around 2200 GMT on Wednesday. He was taken to the hospital where he was treated for a perforated bowel and remains in a serious but stable condition.

Police are investigating three men, aged between 18 and 22, in connection with the attack.

Microsoft has unveiled its next-generation Xbox video game console, set to be released in the US on 22 November. The software giant demonstrated the Xbox 360 at the Consumer Electronics Show in Las Vegas, which can play DVDs and store music, among other features.

The console, which will cost $399, will be released in Europe on 2 December.

Is Halo 2 so popular that it's causing a traffic surge of this magnitude? That's the inference one can draw from this BBC article. The article notes that the increase in traffic could be due to other factors as well.

The same phenomenon was reported in 2003 in Canada, where after the release of Halo 2, Bell Canada reported a 200% increase in internet traffic. This was at a time when they were already offering unlimited internet for a flat fee. Similar situations occurred in 2005 when Rogers Communications reported a 200% increase in traffic after the launch of PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360. Both carriers blamed the new game consoles.
