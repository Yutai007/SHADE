James W. Truman (1863-1942) was an American businessman and philanthropist who built his fortune in the railroad industry, specifically as the president of the Manhattan Elevated Railway Company. Truman was renowned for his generous donations to various causes in New York City, including hospitals, educational institutions, and cultural organizations. His contributions to the city were so significant that his name was immortalized in the James W. Truman Public School on West 123rd Street, which was built in 1913 and continues to serve students to this day. Truman also established the James W. Truman Foundation in 1924, which has since supported education programs in New York City.
