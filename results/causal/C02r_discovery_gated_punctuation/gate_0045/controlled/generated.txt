Mary J. Blige, an American singer, songwriter, and actress, was born in 1971 in the Bronx, New York City. At the age of 11, she began her singing career, and by her late teens, she had signed with Uptown Records. Her debut album, "What's the 411?", was released in 1992 and was a commercial success, with the singles "Real Love" and "Sweet Thing" becoming widespread hits. In the next decade, Blige released a string of successful albums, including "My Life" (1994), "Share My World" (1997), and "Mary" (1999).

Blige's music is known for its raw, emotional style, and she is widely praised for her powerful voice and her ability to convey intense emotions in her songs. She has won multiple Grammy Awards, including Best Female R&B Vocal Performance for the songs "Not Gon' Cry" (1996), "I Can Love You" (1998), and "Heat" (2000). In addition to her music, Blige has had success as an actress, with notable roles in films such as "Rock of Ages" (2012) and "Mudbound" (2017).

Offstage, Blige has been open about her struggles with mental health and addiction, and she has used her platform to raise awareness about these issues. She has been married twice and has one child. Today, Blige continues to record and perform, and she is one of the most successful and influential R&B singers of all time.
