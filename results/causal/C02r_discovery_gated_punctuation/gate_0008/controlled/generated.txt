This paper introduces a novel approach to medical image segmentation using multi-scale self-guided attention. Traditional methods rely on manually designed features or pre-defined templates, which can be limited in their ability to capture complex patterns in medical images. Our approach leverages self-attention mechanisms to learn hierarchical representations of medical images, allowing for effective feature extraction and segmentation at multiple scales.

Our proposed self-guided attention architecture integrates self-attention modules into a multi-scale encoder-decoder framework, enabling the model to adaptively focus on relevant features and regions at different scales. This hierarchical representation learning allows our method to capture both local and global contextual information, leading to improved segmentation accuracy and robustness.

Our method has been evaluated on a variety of medical image modalities, and the results demonstrate its superiority over state-of-the-art methods. Our approach can accurately segment complex structures such as tumors, organs, and vessels, even in the presence of noise, artifacts, and varying image quality. The attention weights generated by our model can be analyzed to provide insights into the hierarchical features and their contribution to segmentation accuracy.

Our work has important implications for medical applications, including disease diagnosis, treatment planning, and monitoring. The proposed method has the potential to improve the efficiency and accuracy of medical image segmentation tasks, ultimately leading to better patient outcomes.
