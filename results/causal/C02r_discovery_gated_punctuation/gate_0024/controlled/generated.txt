As Israel gears up for its first general election since 2019, a critical issue is filling the top management vacancies in the banking sector. Bank of Israel Governor Mark Carney, set to leave in January next year, has announced his intention to leave his position. The finance ministry is now in the process of searching for a new governor with extensive experience in leading large financial institutions and knowledge of international trade regulations. Sources close to the ministry suggest the U.S. is the top candidate, with Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell being a key figure in this process.

Finance Minister Yair Lapid, in a recent visit to the U.S., highlighted the strong and positive relations between Israel and the U.S., emphasizing the need for cooperation based on their long-standing ties. Lapid also mentioned the U.S. has provided Israel with significant support in the past.

The search for a new governor is ongoing, with some senior officials from major investment banks, including JPMorgan Chase, set to interview for the position. It is also believed that other central bankers, such as Andy Haldane and Andrew Bailey of the Bank of England, could also be considered.

Despite ongoing tensions between Israel and the U.S. over Israeli settlement policies, Prime Minister Naftali Bennett has assured that relations remain strong and positive.
