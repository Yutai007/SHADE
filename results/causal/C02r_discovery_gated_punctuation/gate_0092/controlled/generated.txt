This paper explores the transition of a three-dimensional (3D) topological insulator, Bi2Se3, to a two-dimensional (2D) state. Utilizing a blend of experimental and theoretical methods, we analyze Bi2Se3's electronic structure and topological characteristics as its thickness diminishes from 3D to 2D. Our findings reveal that the 3D Dirac cone transforms into a pair of 2D Dirac cones at the edges of the Brillouin zone, leading to the formation of novel topological phases. We observe topologically protected edge states on the surfaces of the 2D system, which are resilient to disorder and defects. Additionally, we identify non-trivial band structure and Fermi arc behavior in the 2D states, indicating the presence of non-trivial topology. This research offers new perspectives on the transition of 3D topological insulators to 2D systems and opens avenues for further investigation into low-dimensional topological phenomena.
