This study introduces two innovative methods for multi-organ segmentation using deep learning. Both techniques utilize the concept of leveraging multiple input planes as parallel channels, allowing different supervision schemes to be defined for different input planes. This approach enables the information flow to better handle ambiguous signals from partial or irrelevant annotations (semi-supervision) as well as to boost the generalization of the model to test images with occluded or rotated organs relative to the training data. Specifically, the proposed methods utilize a combination of a convolutional autoencoder to extract features from the RGB channels (capturing shape variations) and a 3D ResNets to convey anatomical details from a T2w/FLAIR subtraction volume. This approach enables the model to efficiently represent both appearance and geometry through complementary domains, providing significant performance improvements over standard methods using unimodal channels and end-to-end architectures. The study's results show a marked improvement over the performance of the seminal MICCAI publications, with a quantitative comparison on five public datasets demonstrating a performance comparable to that of human experts with the model trained using a different backbone architecture.
