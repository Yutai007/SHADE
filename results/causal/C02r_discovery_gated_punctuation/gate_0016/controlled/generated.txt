The novel chronicles Stevens, a butler at Darlington Hall, who has dedicated his life to serving his master, Lord Darlington. Set in the 1930s, the story begins with Stevens in his mid-50s and has been working at Darlington Hall for over 30 years. He is deeply devoted to his work and takes great pride in it, believing that a butler's role extends beyond mere service to provide continuity and tradition in a rapidly changing world.

Despite his dedication, Stevens faces numerous challenges. He has few close relationships outside of work, and his only companion is his father, who is also a butler at a nearby estate. As the narrative unfolds, Stevens's father becomes ill, forcing him to confront the reality of his own mortality and the choices he has made throughout his life.

Simultaneously, Stevens finds himself entangled in events that challenge his beliefs about his role as a butler and his relationship with his master. Lord Darlington, a wealthy Nazi sympathizer, forces Stevens into confronting moral dilemmas. As political tensions escalate across Europe, Stevens must decide where his loyalties truly lie and what he is willing to do to uphold his sense of duty and honor.

Throughout the novel, Stevens grapples with questions of identity, loyalty, and morality, all against the backdrop of the rapidly evolving world of the 1930s. As he reflects on his life and the decisions he has made, he confronts both personal legacy and broader societal implications. The book offers a poignant and thought-provoking exploration of human nature, raising important questions about duty, loyalty, and identity.
