Scott D. Sullivan, the former chief financial officer of bankrupt US telecom giant WorldCom, has pleaded guilty to three charges in the largest corporate fraud case in US history. Sullivan, 42, admitted to securities fraud, conspiracy to commit securities fraud, and making false filings with the Securities and Exchange Commission. He claimed that he lied about WorldCom's finances to deceive investors, inflating the company's assets by approximately $11 billion between October 2000 and April 2002. Sullivan faces a hefty fine and a maximum prison sentence of 15 years on each of the three counts. His sentencing has been scheduled for late June, and he has agreed to cooperate with prosecutors in the ongoing WorldCom investigation. The guilty plea confirms that Sullivan played a central role in the fraud that led to WorldCom's bankruptcy in 2002. The scandal has deeply impacted the telecommunications industry and generated a crisis of confidence in global financial markets. Other notable figures implicated in the WorldCom fraud case include the company's founder, Bernard Ebbers, who is currently facing charges of securities fraud and conspiracy related to the financial collapse of the company. The fall of WorldCom came during a wave of scandals that shook corporate America and led to a tightening of corporate governance rules under the Sarbanes-Oxley Act.
