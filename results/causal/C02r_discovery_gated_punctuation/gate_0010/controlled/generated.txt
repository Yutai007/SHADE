Scientists have developed a groundbreaking brain-computer interface (BCI) that can read brain signals and control a computer. The device, known as a cap, is worn on the head and uses sensors to detect electrical signals in the brain, which are then translated into commands. The BCI has been tested on volunteers and shown to be accurate and reliable. The technology has the potential to transform lives of people with disabilities, such as those who are unable to use their hands or have difficulty speaking. The BCI is still in the early stages of development, but the researchers are hopeful that it will soon become a viable tool for people with disabilities.
