Selective Publication in Nature: An Empirical Analysis of Abstracts from the Journal of Statistical Genetics Abstract: We investigate selective publication of papers in Nature Genetics. On one side, we analyze the frequency of abstracts in Nature Genetics articles using a logistic regression model, finding significant differences by author, issue, and field. No difference is observed between journals. On the other hand, we compare publications based on geographic location and type (i.e., only or top journals). Similar results are presented. New analyses and possible explanations are discussed. The first part of the analysis uses logistic regression. Hypotheses for potential effects are proposed: journals publish manuscripts of similar quality in the same field, or only manuscripts with no papers or few original articles. In the second part, we analyze manuscripts by author, issue, and region. It shows that patterns differ for abstracts versus no abstracts, controlled for publication status and authorship, and geographical location.

This study will be discussed at a conference in October 2014.
