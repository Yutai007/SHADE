The Earl of Dundonald (Irish: Earra O Domhnaill) was a title in the Peerage of Ireland, created on July 12, 1768, for John Hamilton, who previously represented County Down and Belfast in the Irish House of Commons. He was succeeded by his son, the second Earl. In 1803, he was elevated to the peerage of Great Britain as Baron Belmont, of Castlewellan in the County of Down. On his death, the titles passed to his nephew, the third earl. Born into illegitimacy, Lady Elizabeth Anne Hamilton, eldest daughter of William Henry Hamilton, younger brother of the first Earl, married George Augustus Frederick, Duke of Cambridge, fourth son of King George III. As such, she became Duchess of Cambridge. She died without issue in 1869. Her husband survived her until 1904. On the death of the Duke, the earldom of Dundonald fell into abeyance between his two sisters, Lady Caroline Matilda Augusta FitzClarence and Lady Mary Adelaide Victoria FitzClarence. Both were illegitimate daughters of King William IV. Both died without issue, and the earldom remained in abeyance until 1901, when King Edward VII ascended the throne. The earldom was never called out of abeyance since then.
