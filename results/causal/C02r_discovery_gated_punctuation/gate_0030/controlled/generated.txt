The South African government has announced plans to reopen its embassy in Mauritius, ending a 20-year diplomatic standoff between the two countries.

The decision was made following talks between President Cyril Ramaphosa and Prime Minister Pravind Jugnauth, who met in Ethiopia to discuss the African Union summit. Mauritius had accused South Africa of supporting separatist rebels in the 1980s and 1990s, leading to the closure of the South African embassy in Port Louis in 1997.

Relations between the two nations have improved significantly over recent years, with both sides working together to combat piracy and promote regional stability. South Africa's foreign ministry said that the reopening of the embassy would help strengthen bilateral ties and boost economic cooperation between the two nations.

"We believe that this is an important step towards normalising our relationship," said spokesperson Clayson Mbatha. "It will allow us to engage more actively with each other on issues of mutual interest."

Mauritius welcomed the move, saying it hoped to deepen its engagement with South Africa and benefit from increased trade and investment. "This is a positive development for our region as a whole," said Foreign Affairs Minister Alain St. Ange. "We look forward to further strengthening our partnership with South Africa."
