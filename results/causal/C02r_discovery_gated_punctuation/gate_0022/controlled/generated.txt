This paper introduces a topological image segmentation technique for automatic sign language recognition. It employs the watershed transformation to derive the topological derivative from the gradient magnitude of video frames. However, the gradient magnitude does not meet the topological derivative's requirements, so an isotropic filter is applied to normalize its values. This approach enhances the watershed-based segmentation technique's effectiveness and robustness. Subsequently, regions are classified using statistical features from the normalized RGB histogram. Feature selection is then performed using the Fisher score to reduce dimensionality and identify the most relevant features for classification. Finally, the k-NN classifier is used for the classification of gesture sequences. The proposed method has been validated using the Texas Instrument DLP Sign Language Gesture Database, achieving a recognition rate of 95.6%.
