It starts with the introduction of a new town. It is a little quaint, and people look healthy. The book is about its residents and their lives compared to how things used to be.

The novel is about a fictional detective's time and experience. He is referred to as "Detective Caul" and begins with him and his friend discovering a body in the woods. They call the police on themselves and their friend, who tells them that Caul might not be guilty. The detectives ask Caul to meet them at a tavern, but he refuses, implying he has a case. After some time, Caul shows up to the tavern and asks if the man who discovered the body is still involved in the case. Afterward, the detectives agree to talk to him before arresting Caul, but when Caul gets information, he is shot in the head, solving the mystery. However, the book does not reveal who killed Caul, only hints towards the suspect. Then it jumps to another book, where the killer is revealed, murdered similarly to Caul's friend, leading to trial. Caul is acquitted, and the narrator says it should have ended like it did. The book ends with Caul telling his story to a class, asking two questions: did he kill his friend? Was a man already involved in one murder also responsible for killing Caul?
