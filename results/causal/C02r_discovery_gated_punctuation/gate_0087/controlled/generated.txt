Recently, several methods based on transformer architecture have been proposed in biomedical image segmentation. Transformer architectures have shown great promise in various tasks, including natural language processing and computer vision. However, most existing transformer-based methods for image segmentation only consider a single image at a time, limiting their capacity in modeling long-range dependencies. In this paper, we propose a multi-compound transformer model for accurate biomedical image segmentation. Our model consists of two components: a feature extraction module and a segmentation module. The feature extraction module is a transformer-based Encoder-Decoder architecture, which extracts deep feature representations from images. The segmentation module is composed of a transformer-based Encoder and a Bi-LSTM decoder. The transformer-based Encoder captures long-range dependencies in the feature representations, while the Bi-LSTM decoder generates final segmentation masks. Experimental results show that our method outperforms state-of-the-art approaches by significant margins when evaluated on publicly available datasets such as ISBI challenge and BraTS challenge.
