Prime Minister Boris Johnson announced his resignation from his position in June, marking the end of his three-year leadership of the United Kingdom.

In a statement delivered outside 10 Downing Street, Johnson informed the Queen of his decision earlier in the day. He expressed gratitude for the opportunity to serve and acknowledged the weight of responsibility that comes with being prime minister.

"It has been an immense privilege to serve as your prime minister," Johnson said. "I have always tried my best to live up to the trust placed in me by the British people and to do what I believe is right for our country."

Johnson's departure comes amid mounting pressure following a series of scandals and controversies that have marred his time in office. A growing number of Conservative MPs had called for his resignation, citing concerns over his leadership style and handling of key issues, including Brexit and the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic.

The Prime Minister's announcement was met with mixed reactions from politicians and the public. Some colleagues praised Johnson's commitment to public service, while critics welcomed his departure. Opposition leaders called for a general election, arguing that the country needs a new leadership and a new direction.

The timeline for Johnson's departure is unclear, but it is expected that he will officially step down at the end of June. The process for selecting a new Conservative Party leader – and by extension, the next prime minister – will likely begin shortly thereafter. Several names are already circulating as potential successors, including prominent cabinet members such as Chancellor Rishi Sunak and Home Secretary Priti Patel.

As the United Kingdom prepares for the transition, many are reflecting on Johnson's legacy. Some supporters point to some of his notable achievements during his time in office, such as securing a Brexit withdrawal agreement with the European Union.
