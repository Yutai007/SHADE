Leslie S. Klinger is an American author renowned for her work in the mystery and thriller genres. Born in New York City in 1967, Klinger grew up in the suburbs of New Jersey and attended the University of Pennsylvania, where she earned a degree in English literature.

Klinger's first novel, "Death by Station Wagon," was published in 1996 and introduced the character of Claire Waldstein, a suburban mother turned amateur detective. The book was well-received, and Klinger went on to write several more novels featuring Waldstein, including "Death by PTA" and "Death by Gymnastics."

In addition to the Waldstein series, Klinger has written several standalone novels, including "The Red House," "The Dead Girl's Dance," and "The Meaning of Glass." Her work has been praised for its clever plots, well-drawn characters, and dark humor.

Klinger has won several awards for her writing, including the Agatha Award and the Anthony Award. She has also been a finalist for the Edgar Award, and her work has been translated into several languages.

Klinger currently lives in New Jersey with her husband and two children. When she's not writing, she enjoys reading, hiking, and spending time with her family.
