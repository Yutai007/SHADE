Segmentation is a crucial task in biomedical image analysis. Its quality depends on prior knowledge or domain expertise, and is also constrained by the feature learning capabilities of deep learning-based methods. In this paper, a novel semi-supervised clustering and deep feature learning method is proposed for 3D medical image segmentation. To integrate the unsupervised segmentation from a deep autoencoder method and the manual labeling provided by experts, the proposed approach jointly utilizes segmentation labels and their corresponding features through clustering and unsupervised feature extraction techniques. Specifically, it consists of two steps: first, images are processed with an autoencoder model to generate image feature representations for each image; second, semi-supervised clustering is performed on the feature representations of the images to simultaneously obtain dense image labels and the centroid of each cluster in feature space. Finally, deep feature learning is applied to update the image feature representations. This iterative process continues until convergence. Experimental results on the cardiovascular dataset demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms current state-of-the-art deep learning-based segmentation approaches in terms of F1-score.
