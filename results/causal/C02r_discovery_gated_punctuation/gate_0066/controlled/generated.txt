Italy's Parmalat founder Calisto Tanzi admits to accounting errors at the dairy firm he led for three decades, apologizing for "infinite damage" caused. The firm collapsed in December when it admitted falsifying accounts by 15 billion euros. Tanzi's empire is being sold to Brazilian rival Vigor Alimentos and Asian assets are being sold. He is seeking to find solutions for the firm's future and to make reparations, but the group cannot be bailed out. Tanzi's statements are in the wake of news of some investors being given interest-free loans in the past week. Parmalat, Italy's largest food company, was forced into administration in December when it failed to raise the promised funds for a $500 billion bond repayment. At a recent meeting with Prime Minister Silvio Berlusconi and other top politicians, Tanzi admitted "errors and non-transparency" in accounting practices. Berlusconi demanded more and called Tanzi's statements "insufficient," with the prime minister threatening to ban Tanzi from other Italian companies for up to four years.
