The Fife Coalfield was a significant coal mining region in Scotland, primarily located in Fife and extending into neighboring counties of Angus and Kinross. Historically, it played a crucial role in the economic development of Fife, providing employment for many years.

The first coal mine in the area was established in 1575, and the industry experienced rapid growth during the 18th and 19th centuries. At its peak, the Fife Coalfield boasted over 300 mines, making it one of the most productive coal mining regions in the United Kingdom.

The Fife Coalfield began to decline in the early 20th century due to increasing competition from other energy sources. The last coal mine in the area closed in 1966, marking the end of the industry. Today, the Fife Coalfield is remembered as an important part of Fife's history, with numerous artifacts and monuments commemorating the industry. The Scottish Mining Museum in Newtongrange attracts many visitors, and the area is also home to several heritage sites dedicated to the coal miners of the Fife Coalfield.
