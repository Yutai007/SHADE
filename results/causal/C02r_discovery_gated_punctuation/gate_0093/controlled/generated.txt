Nigeria plans to significantly boost its cocoa production over the next decade, aiming to increase its cocoa bean output by 50% by 2030, according to President Muhammadu Buhari. The country aims to remove barriers to irrigation and fertilizer use, with current annual cocoa production at 340,000 tonnes, making it the world's fourth-largest producer behind Ivory Coast, Indonesia, and Ghana. The president has pledged to make Nigeria the world's top cocoa producer. However, critics argue that past efforts have failed, and the government needs to clarify if the promised increase will be achieved by converting rainforest land into cocoa farms or by intensifying fertilizer use. Nigeria's tropical forests, home to endangered species like chimps and pangolins, are threatened by deforestation, and the government has previously promised to return 1 million hectares of land to forests annually, but few measures have been implemented.
